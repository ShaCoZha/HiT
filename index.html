Abstract

Inertial sensors in glasses, watches, and phones observe human motion all day, yet existing models either estimate pose from a fixed, calibrated sensor setup or map the signals directly to activity labels and text without an explicit model of the body. We present HiT (Human Inertial Transformer), a unified model that estimates full-body pose from any subset of body-worn IMUs and describes the observed motion in natural language without calibration or sensor placement knowledge. We first train HiT on real-world motion capture to estimate pose and the placement of every sensor, then freeze it and output language from it with a language head supervised jointly by activity labels, narration alignment, and captioning. Trained only on Nymeria, HiT raises zero-shot activity recognition accuracy across ten unseen datasets from 43.9% (AnyMo) to 65.1%, improves IMU–text retrieval MRR 1.8- to 2.2-fold on the full Nymeria test pool and on zero-shot Ego-Exo4D, and raises captioning BLEU-4 from 6.5 to 11.0. Without T-pose calibration and without knowing the sensor sites, its pose estimates are competitive with calibrated methods across datasets, more accurate than IMU4D zero-shot on IMUPoser and DIP-IMU; it also recognizes the body segment of 93.6% of the sensors on unseen sequences.

Method

A body model first, language on top

Stage 1

Pose and placement from raw signals

Every sensor gives its own specific force and angular velocity, in its own frame. HiT is trained on real motion capture (Nymeria: 17 suit sensors, glasses and wristbands, 213 hours) with random mounting rotations and sensors moved around each limb, so it learns where a sensor sits from how it moves. It outputs the full-body pose and, for every sensor, its body segment out of 17.

Stage 2

Language grounded in the body

The body model is frozen. A language head reads it and is trained with three signals at once: activity labels, alignment with narrations, and captioning with a small LLM. The words therefore come from the motion the model has reconstructed, not from a shortcut between signals and text.

Zero-shot activity recognition

Activities it was never trained on

Trained only on Nymeria, HiT recognizes the activities of ten public datasets from their class names alone, with whatever sensors each dataset has, and without being told where they are worn.
